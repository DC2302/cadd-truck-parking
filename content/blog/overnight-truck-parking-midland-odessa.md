<p>If you run the Permian, you already know the math: more trucks than anywhere in West Texas, and nowhere near enough legal places to shut down for the night. Your clock says stop — federal hours-of-service rules don't care whether you found a spot or not. So where can you actually put seventy-plus feet of truck and trailer when the day's done?</p>

<p>Here's the honest rundown.</p>

<h2>The spots that look free (and what they cost)</h2>

<p><strong>Rest areas.</strong> Legal, yes — but TxDOT rest areas post time limits, fill up early on busy corridors, and offer exactly zero security. If you've pulled into one at 9 p.m. on I-20, you know the odds of finding an open slot.</p>

<p><strong>Retail lots and truck stop fringes.</strong> Some stores tolerate trucks; many don't, and the "No Overnight Parking" signs mean tow trucks in some lots. Even where it's tolerated, you're the guest of a business that can change its mind at midnight — and nobody's watching your load.</p>

<p><strong>Shoulders, ramps, and lease roads.</strong> This is where tired drivers end up when everything else is full, and it's the worst option on the list. Parking on highway shoulders and ramps is generally prohibited except for emergencies, it's where trucks get hit, and out here a "quiet" caliche pullout is somebody's active lease road at 4 a.m. A ticket is the cheap version of how that night goes wrong.</p>

<p><strong>The street in town.</strong> Most cities in the Basin restrict big-truck parking in residential and many commercial areas. Rules change block to block and get enforced in waves — always check current local ordinances instead of betting on last month's habit.</p>

<h2>What "free" parking really costs</h2>

<ul>
<li><strong>Your sleep.</strong> One ear open all night listening for door handles isn't rest — and rest is the whole point of the stop.</li>
<li><strong>Your equipment and load.</strong> Fuel theft, catalytic converters, cut curtains, missing straps. Ask any driver who's parked "wherever" in the oil patch long enough — they've got a story or know someone who does.</li>
<li><strong>Your record.</strong> Tickets and tows follow you; so does explaining a load claim to a broker.</li>
<li><strong>Your morning.</strong> No shower, no bathroom, gas-station coffee, and a scramble to get legal again. That's a rough way to start a 14-hour clock.</li>
</ul>

<h2>What to look for in a real overnight spot</h2>

<p>Whether it's our lot or anybody else's, a spot worth paying for should check these boxes:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Fenced and gated</strong> — one way in, one way out, not an open field.</li>
<li><strong>Cameras that actually record</strong>, and somebody who answers the phone.</li>
<li><strong>An assigned space</strong> — so you're not circling at midnight hoping.</li>
<li><strong>Room to maneuver</strong> a full-length combination without threading needles.</li>
<li><strong>The human stuff</strong>: real bathrooms, a hot shower, laundry, a place to sit that isn't your cab. Recovery is part of the job.</li>
</ul>
